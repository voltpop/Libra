Libra v0 (beta)

A handheld, open-hardware device that is an OTP manager, an open security key (FIDO2 + OpenPGP card), and an in-person GPG key-signing tool, and an encrypting storage broker, with a screen, a rear camera and a hold-to-approve button.

Target MCU
ESP32-S3 module
Interfaces
Screen, buttons, camera, fingerprint, USB-C, microSD, BLE
Primary control
PTT: tap / hold to commit
Licence intent
CERN-OHL hardware, open firmware
How to read this report. Parts, pin assignments, library names and figures are proposals. Anything marked verify could not be confirmed and is a task to check against datasheets before design freeze. Pin numbers in particular must be validated against the exact module datasheet.

At a glance

What it does

  • Scan a QR to enrol TOTP/HOTP accounts
  • Act as a FIDO2 / OpenPGP security key over USB
  • Exchange and certify GPG keys face to face
  • Decrypt and sign on the desktop through the OpenPGP card
  • Encrypt storage on the fly: an SD card or USB stick appears to the host as a normal drive (the Vault)
  • Candidates: Bitcoin PSBT signer and a shareable identity card

Key design choices

  • ESP32-S3, 2.0" IPS screen, rear camera; open hardware and firmware
  • Face: D-pad, Select, Back. Top edge: one recessed PTT (tap = harmless, hold = commit)
  • Hold-to-approve on a trusted display; PIN entered on the device; goal: unlock GPG with your fingerprint
  • Two microSD slots (System and Vault) and a second USB port that encrypts a plugged-in stick (a v0 goal, optional in v1)
  • Fingerprint module (required); optional secure element; crypto profiles with hardware-limit alerts
  • v0 on a breadboard with everything in scope; v1 pared down and designed with a designer

Deliberately out

  • NFC fistbump (replaced by QR + BLE)
  • Photo / face biometrics
  • Keyserver and TLS logic on the device
  • Tamper-resistance claims the hardware cannot back
